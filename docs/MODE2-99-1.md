# Mode 2: the non-custodial 99/1 coinbase split

Mode 2 is what makes this pool different from every "solo pool" that keeps a balance for you. There is no
balance. The block reward is split **inside the coinbase transaction**, by the protocol, at the moment the
block is assembled.

## The transaction layout

When a miner's share solves a block, the coinbase transaction contains, in order:

| Output | Amount | Paid to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `99%` of the miner reward | the `t1…`/`t3…` address embedded in the worker name (`<address>.<worker>`) |
| 2 | `1%` (`mode2.fee_pct`) | the pool's `pool_fee_address` from `config.json` |

With a block reward of 1.5625 ZEC that is 1.546875 ZEC to the miner and 0.015625 ZEC to the pool — both
visible in the same transaction, both spendable by the recipient the moment the block matures. The pool
operator never has an intermediate account, and there is nothing to withdraw, freeze or seize.

The coinbase also carries the value-pool accounting required by the block template. On current Zcash
mainnet that includes the NU6 development-fund commitment to the protocol **lockbox** (`0.125 ZEC` per
block at the time of writing), which is paid by the protocol into an address no party controls — it is not
part of the miner/pool split above.

## Why this is safer than a custodial pool

1. **No custodial float.** A traditional pool accumulates shares, computes a payout table and sends coins
   later. That float is the risk: hacks, exit scams, frozen operations, or an operator who simply stops
   paying. Here there is no float to protect.
2. **No payout ledger to dispute.** The chain is the ledger. If your address is in the coinbase, you were
   paid.
3. **Fee transparency.** The 1% is not deducted from a balance you have to take on faith — it is an output
   in a transaction you can inspect before and after it confirms.
4. **No account.** The username is the payout address. There is nothing to register, no email, no KYC.

## What the engine validates before it will mine for you

- The worker name must parse as `<Zcash address>.<worker name>` and pass base58check + network validation.
  A malformed address is rejected at `mining.authorize` with `Invalid ZEC address`; the engine will not
  build a template it cannot pay.
- Shares are checked against the current job at the difficulty assigned to that worker (start difficulty
  512, adapted by vardiff to a target of 8–15 shares/minute).
- Stale shares (submitted against a job whose previous block hash is no longer the tip) are counted as
  rejects, not silently discarded.

Pool-internal test rigs can bypass per-miner coinbase construction by using a worker name with one of the
prefixes in `mode2.whitelist_prefixes` (default `local.`). Those sessions mine to the node-level template
instead of their own address — use it only for your own testing, never for a real miner.

## Failure modes we test for

| Failure | Expected behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Worker name is not a valid address | authorisation is refused, connection closed, nothing mined |
| Worker disconnects mid-job | session is dropped; no partial credit exists to lose |
| New block arrives while a share is in flight | the old job is invalidated (`clean_jobs`), the stale share is counted as rejected |
| `zebrad` RPC restarts | the engine retries; miners keep their session, jobs resume when a template is available |
| Pool process restarts | share counters are restored from `counters.json`; miners simply reconnect |

## Verifying it yourself

1. Point a rig at the pool with `t1YourAddress.rig1` as the username.
2. Watch `worker_list` in the status file: your shares and difficulty appear under that exact worker string.
3. When the pool finds a block, fetch the coinbase transaction from any explorer and compare the outputs
   with the table above. The 99/1 split is the proof — there is no other accounting to trust.
